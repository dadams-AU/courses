THE CLOSING LINE IS THE POINT OF THE SLIDE, and it is a policy-feedback story two weeks before Pierson. Large-bank overdraft revenue halved because banks cut fees, added grace periods, and dropped NSF charges under regulatory pressure starting in late 2021. The CFPB then finalized a rule in December 2024 capping most overdraft fees at $5. Congress killed it under the Congressional Review Act; signed May 9, 2025, before the rule ever took effect. The CFPB's own finding, in the same report as the $5.83 billion: banks "appear to have stopped significantly reducing overdraft fees." So the sequence is pressure, retreat, repeal, plateau. Ask the room who the constituency was at each step.
