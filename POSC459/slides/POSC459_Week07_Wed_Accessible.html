1965: the afterthought

  • Medicare: the headline. Universal, for the old
  • Medicaid: the supplement. Means-tested, run by the states
  • Wilbur Mills’s bet: cover the “worthy” poor and

“stave off claims for broader health-care coverage” (p. 339)

Three seeds of expansion

Seed What it lets a state do
Medical indigency Cover people who aren’t poor but can’t pay the bill
Comprehensive benefits Pay for nearly any service
A generous federal match Spend a dollar, get 50 to 75 cents back

Grogan and Andrews, p. 339

Then Waxman

Rep. Henry Waxman (D-CA), health subcommittee chair, 1979–1994

1984–87 Options: states may cover more pregnant women and children
1988–90 Mandates: states must
1990 All poor children under 18, phased in through 2002

“the Waxman wedge” (Howard, p. 103)

The test case: workers’ compensation

  • First social insurance program: 42 states by 1920
  • No federal money, no federal rules
  • $50 billion in benefits (2001), bigger than any means-tested program but Medicaid
  • Death benefit in Virginia: three times North Carolina’s

Howard, pp. 154–155, 159
