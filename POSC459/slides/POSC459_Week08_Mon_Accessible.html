What the line counts, and what it can’t

  • Orshansky, 1965: a cheap food budget × 3, cash income only
  • Official rate 12.6% (1970) · 15.1% (2010) · 10.5% (2019). Spending per person $1,015 → $3,419 (1980–2017)
  • Never counted: SNAP, the EITC, housing aid. Child poverty, supplemental measure, 9.7% → 5.2% → 12.4% (2020–22)
  • The leak: TANF, 22 cents of the dollar reaches a family as cash

Weeks 4 and 5 · Pierson 1993, figure 1 · Berkowitz and DeWitt

Social Security and Medicare: the bet, the hedge, the fallback

  • 1935: payroll financing, the bet. Old Age Assistance, means-tested and state-run, the hedge
  • 1948: the hedge pays 50 percent more. Then 1950 coverage · 1972 indexing · the 1983 rescue
  • Medicare, 1965: Ball’s “fallback” after national health insurance lost. The price: pay providers what they asked

“the nation’s most important antipoverty program, in part because it did not target its benefits exclusively to the poor” (p. 271)

Week 7 · Howard ch. 8 · Grogan and Andrews

A blue-book answer has four parts

Claim Answer the question in one sentence. Make it the first sentence
Concept Name it, in the author’s term: lock-in, layering, the match, the hidden welfare state
Evidence A case, a number, a year. “Social Security down 1.5 percent, AFDC 16”
So what What the case shows that the concept predicted, or didn’t

Weak: everything you remember about the topic. Strong: one argument, two pieces of evidence, a name on each.
