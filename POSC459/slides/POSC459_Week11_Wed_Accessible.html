1. Welfare reform: no one organized to defend AFDC

  • Social Security, Medicare: a powerful constituency
  • Welfare recipients “lacked a strong political presence”
  • Medicaid’s providers have a stake; AFDC had none

Béland and Waddan, p. 69 · Week 9

TANF shrank, and nobody came back for it

What Number
Eligible families reached 84% (1995) → 40% (2005)
2007–09 recession Unemployment doubled; caseload +13%
Block grant $16.5 billion since 1996 (outside the readings)

“No impetus to roll back the changes”

Béland and Waddan, pp. 67–68 · CRS IF10036

Ending welfare didn’t change views of the poor

  • Welfare as a top problem: 26.6% (1996) → 4.8% (2000)
  • Poor are too dependent on aid, 2003: 71%
  • Distant-visible: a spectacle few experience

“Erasure, however, is not the same as counter-argument”

Soss and Schram, pp. 116, 122–123 · open to all for Paper 4

2. The ACA: benefits went to groups with lobbyists

Who Gained
The uninsured 26 million more covered
Hospitals Medicaid pays for the uninsured
Health industry 32 million new customers; “winners, on balance”

Jacobs and Skocpol, chs. 2 and 4

The ACA was visible and traceable: that cut both ways

  • “ObamaCare”: more disapproved than approved
  • Its core features: 60% to 80% support
  • Pierson: voters react to what they can see and trace

Jacobs and Skocpol, ch. 4, p. 1 · Pierson, p. 622

The ACA delayed the pain, as Pierson predicted

“front-load benefits and back-load costs” (Pierson, p. 622)

Provision What happened
Coverage, Medicaid, insurer rules Still law
Mandate penalty Repeal fails, July 2017; $0 from 2019

Jacobs and Skocpol, p. 17 · outside the readings: Senate; IRS

3. The tax credit: housing built through investors’ tax bills

  • IRS → states → developers → investors, mostly banks
  • About $10 billion a year (Howard); $8 billion (film)
  • “‘Low-Income’ does not mean poor”

Howard ch. 7, Canvas PDF p. 9 · film

Its defenders get what Pierson calls “spoils”

  • An industry convention in Chicago
  • Investors: “a good after-tax yield”
  • Howard: developers and landlords as caregivers

Film · Howard, Canvas PDF p. 11 · Pierson, p. 599

Who can see it? Almost no one, and that protects it

  • Tax expenditures: no yearly review by Congress
  • “There’s only been seven audits in 29 years” (Grassley)
  • Treasury declined an interview

Pierson, p. 620 · film
